TL;DR

Both Zola and Reelday offer a free wedding website with RSVP tracking. The difference is localization: Zola is a US product with a registry-and-vendor ecosystem bolted on; Reelday is free-forever and built specifically for Philippine weddings — Tagalog/English toggle, GCash for the optional paid upgrade, entourage sections, and a find-your-seat tool that only reveals a guest's own table. Reelday also adds an optional live guest photo & video wall for the reception itself, which Zola doesn't have at all.

Why couples look for a Zola alternative

Zola is a genuinely well-built product — free website, free RSVP, and a large gift-registry business behind it. For a US wedding, it's a reasonable default. For a Philippine wedding, the gaps show up fast: guest-facing text defaults to English only, there's no built-in way to handle a PH-style entourage (principal sponsors, secondary sponsors, godparents), and there's no local payment rail if a couple ever wants to upgrade to something more. Some couples also find Zola's registry-first design distracting when what they actually need is a simple, elegant invitation site.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a Zola alternative built for the Philippines?

Yes — Reelday is a free wedding website builder made specifically for Filipino couples, with Tagalog/English support, GCash payment for optional upgrades, and PH-specific conventions like entourage lists and find-your-seat, none of which Zola offers.

Does Zola work for weddings in the Philippines?

Zola works technically, but it's built US-first — no Tagalog option, no local payment methods, and no PH wedding conventions like entourage sections or table-based seating. Couples often end up customizing it heavily or looking elsewhere.

Is Reelday really free like Zola?

Yes, the wedding website itself — RSVP, countdown, story, details, find-your-seat — is free forever, no credit card required. Reelday makes money on an optional paid upgrade: a live guest photo & video wall for the reception, not on the website.
